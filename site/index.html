Developing Quantum Use Cases from Future Everyday Life Scenarios

This Birds-of-a-Feather session uses future everyday-life stories to help participants identify desired changes, explore where quantum technologies might contribute, and connect emerging use cases with technical expertise and interdisciplinary collaboration.

Motivation and objectives

Start from life, not from a predefined technology problem

Starting point

Desired changes in everyday life

Participants consider burdens, constraints, activities, and experiences they would like to change, realize, or explore further.

Exploration

Possible quantum contributions

Short future-life stories help participants discuss where quantum computing, networking, sensing, or hybrid systems might become relevant.

Outcome

Shared questions and connections

The session connects visions, technical capabilities, domain expertise, concerns, and opportunities for interdisciplinary collaboration.

Guiding question

The question organizing the session

Where and how might quantum technologies contribute to changes in everyday life that participants would like to see realized or explored further?

90-minute session

Five phases from framing to reflection

  1. 01.

    Framing and audience engagement

    Purpose, guiding question, examples, and live polling.

    10 min
  2. 02.

    Story-based individual exploration

    Select a future-life story and record responses, keywords, diagrams, or sketches.

    15 min
  3. 03.

    Small-group discussion

    Share ideas across technical and professional backgrounds.

    20 min
  4. 04.

    Plenary discussion

    Examine selected ideas, possible quantum contributions, reasons, and open questions.

    35 min
  5. 05.

    Reflection and closing

    Identify an insight or topic to explore further.

    10 min

Individual and plenary exploration

Prompts that move from stories to use cases

1.

Individual prompts

  • Which change in the story is most significant?
  • What change would you like to see realized or explored?
  • Where might quantum technologies be relevant?
2.

Plenary perspectives

  • Change in everyday life
  • Possible contribution of quantum technologies
  • Reasons a capability might be relevant
  • Technical, practical, and social questions

Future-story development

Three examples of everyday-life change

01.

Less conscious coordination

Scheduling, delivery, mobility, and service availability are coordinated in the background, reducing manual negotiation, waiting, and logistical constraints.

Possible relation: combinatorial optimization and quantum algorithms
02.

Less constraint from physical distance

People live, work, collaborate, and manage distant environments without being strongly constrained by geographical proximity.

Possible relation: quantum sensing for remote and sensory-rich experiences
03.

Less conscious energy management

Everyday devices and mobility systems obtain energy from surrounding sources and local infrastructure without routine charging management.

Possible relation: quantum simulation for materials and chemistry research

Stories combine fictional characters, familiar situations, products or services, and technological and social assumptions. They are designed for open discussion rather than prediction.

Audience and organizers

A meeting point for different forms of expertise

Who should join

Researchers, engineers, practitioners, and students interested in quantum applications and everyday life - including hardware, software, networking, hybrid systems, application domains, business, policy, governance, and education.

No use case needs to be prepared in advance.
