Story 1

Traffic and Energy Coordination in a Smart City During Extreme Heat

Read the story, explore each scene, and discuss what kind of future you would like to see.

Step 1

Explore the Story

Scene 1

Social Context

In this future, the calculation and public disclosure of an international indicator linking the adoption of decarbonized materials with reductions in electricity consumption across production, transport, and use became mandatory worldwide.

Companies and cities must demonstrate not only that they have introduced these materials, but also how they contribute to city-wide electricity reduction targets each year.

A city-wide integrated management system introduced by governments and electricity providers now coordinates factory operations, logistics deliveries, public transport, and the electricity consumed through residents’ mobility. As a result, it has become more difficult for companies and residents to determine production schedules and travel routes solely according to their own circumstances.

Scene 2

Collective Behavioral Change

Previously, delivery drivers checked the goods and destinations assigned to them and selected routes in response to road conditions.

Now, most delivery and reporting processes are automated. When student drivers register their available time, their working hours, vehicle, and delivery area are automatically assigned.

They drive energy-efficient delivery vehicles developed using the results of computational materials discovery. They follow delivery sequences and routes calculated by the city-wide integrated management system based on delivery deadlines, vehicle locations, battery levels, roads, charging facilities, and available electricity.

Their driving and operational data are also used to improve autonomous driving systems.

Scene 3

Change in Individual Decision-Making

On an extremely hot afternoon, Taro drives along the route designated by the city-wide integrated management system. He wants to complete his deliveries without worsening his physical condition while maintaining the income and future work opportunities he depends on.

Taro receives tuition support and a grant-based scholarship, and uses his delivery income to cover living expenses. Any reduction in pay or future work assignments would directly affect his life the following month.

During the delivery, he becomes intensely thirsty, and his wearable device recommends that he rest. However, the next designated rest point is more than twenty minutes away, and no additional break is included in the current plan.

A nearby water station can only be reached by leaving the designated route. The delivery system indicates that doing so will reduce his pay and lower his route-compliance score.

Taro chooses to leave the route and go to the water station. He records his symptoms, the warning from his wearable device, and the system notification in order to challenge a system that treats health-related rest as an ordinary route violation.

Scene 4

Secondary Issues

The city-wide integrated management system has reduced fluctuations in basic living costs such as electricity and transport, even when weather conditions and electricity demand change. This has reduced some of the stress associated with everyday life.

The system calculates delivery plans using external conditions such as electricity supply and demand, weather, demand for goods, and supply-chain conditions.

However, individual conditions and circumstances—including fatigue, physical ability, health, interests, and personal responsibilities—are not adequately reflected in the plans.

Connecting wearable and biometric information to the city system could enable more adaptive planning. At the same time, doing so could expand the scope of monitoring and raise new questions about privacy, fairness, consent, and the use of personal data.
